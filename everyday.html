Everyday Mood Log App

Objective

Would you change your habits if you had evidence it would lead to better days?



Everyday is an exploration of a mood log app focused on understanding the impact of our habits and how they can be leveraged them to build better days. While this is a personal project, the design and strategy are informed by several rounds of user research and my years of professional experience in healthcare.

    • Original concept

    This idea came to me while I was working toward my Bachelor's degree, and it started not as an app, but a spreadsheet. Curious if there were any patterns to be found between my habits (particularly my college student sleep schedule) and mood, I logged both for several months in Excel, building charts and tables to assess the connections.



    Seeing the data empowered me to build healthier habits and I was able to meaningfully raise my average mood over the months I maintained the log. The spreadsheet did become tedious, of course, but the value of the experience made a lasting impression.

    Initial research

    The first round of discovery was conducted with college-age adults to understand what UX patterns help with habit-forming and what types of habits people anticipated wanting to track.



    The biggest learning from this research was that people have needs and preferences that vary widely when it comes to using a habit-tracking app. When asked when they felt they would be most likely to use a habit/mood app in a day, participant responses were split between morning, evening, and throughout the day. Preferred habits to track varied even further.



    Despite those differences, a few common themes did emerge from this initial research. Participants agreed the app needed to have little to no friction, and most people said some indication of a "streak" is motivating when trying to form a new habit.
